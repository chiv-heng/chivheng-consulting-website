Key Tensions

60-Day Kill Switch vs. 9-Month Gate
Most advisors want a 60-day sales sprint. Wendy Kopp argues K-12 procurement doesn't move in 60 days — districts need board approvals, vendor vetting, budget cycles. Resolution: measure interest (LOIs, pilots) at 60 days, not closed revenue.
Tobi/Jason/April/Rob/Sam (60d) vs. Wendy (9mo) vs. Sal (30d)
Builder's Comfort Zone vs. Sales Reality
The founder has spent 11 milestones building without attempting to sell. Every advisor flagged this pattern. Is it avoidance of the hard part (selling), or is it evidence the market isn't pulling?
Jason Fried ("hobby") & Sal Khan ("absence of pull is a signal") — two sides of the same coin
Joy vs. Expected Value
CVP scores 8/10 on founder joy; Staffing Studio scores 5-6/10. Multiple advisors argued joy from greenfield building fades fast once you hit distribution walls. But Wendy Kopp's joy floor (below 4/10 = reassess) acknowledges burnout is a real strategic risk.
Sal Khan (follow the joy) vs. Jason Fried (joy is a trap) vs. Wendy Kopp (joy has a floor)

Full Advisor Arguments

Geoffrey Canada — Domain Expert

Core argument: Pipeline thinking — fixing staffing operations unlocks instructional leadership, which drives student outcomes. "Every week a position sits unfilled, kids lose."

On CVP: "The families you want to serve with the College Visit Planner — those are my kids. The thing that gets those kids to college is having great teachers in their schools right now."

Key rebuttal: Challenged Sal Khan's viral adoption comparison — Khan Academy had years, philanthropic backing, and zero monetization pressure. "That is not analogous. That is aspirational to the point of being dangerous advice."

Sam Hinkie — Adjacent Visionary

Core argument: Expected value analysis. 15-25 schools at $3-10K each vs. 640 families at $125 each. "The denominator matters." Probability: 30-45% (SS) vs 5-15% (CVP).

Framework: Optionality preservation — build CVP later from a position of strength. "Options increase in value with time and information."

Key rebuttal: Called Sal's $50-200K CVP projection "not a forecast, it's a wish." Endorsed Tobi's kill switch as "Tetlock-grade forecasting discipline."

Jason Fried — Contrarian

Core argument: "Stop building. Start selling. Today. This week." The product is done. The gap is 50 phone calls.

Sharpest line: "The hardest part of a solo founder's journey isn't building. It's the moment you stop building and start putting your work in front of people who might say no."

On CVP joy: "That joy lasts about 6 weeks. Then you're back to the same grind, except now you're in a harder market."

Tobi Lutke — Radical Thinker

Core argument: 60-day kill switch. Freeze features, pure sales. 3 paying customers by Day 60 or sunset cleanly. "Code without customers is inventory, not product-market fit."

Framework: Compounding and leverage — domain knowledge encoded in 113K LOC is real leverage, but only if activated by customers.

April Dunford — Operator

Core argument: Positioning problem, not product problem. Competitive alternative is spreadsheets (inertia, not software). 20 discovery calls to reframe the value proposition.

CVP positioning analysis: "Free alternatives are good enough. A $99/year subscription for something you use for 6-8 months of your life? That is a tough sell."

Most cited insight: "Spreadsheets are the real competitor" — referenced by 5 other advisors as the most actionable reframe.

Wendy Kopp — Impact Leader

Core argument: Systems change vs. point solution. Staffing Studio touches budgets, compliance, HR, and instructional planning simultaneously. CVP solves one moment.

Dissent on timeline: 9-month gate, not 60 days. "Education moves at the speed of trust." TFA took 18 months to formalize first district partnership — and they were offering teachers for free.

Unique contribution: Joy floor — if founder joy drops below 4/10, reassess regardless of traction.

Rob Walling — Financial Strategist

Core argument: B2C freemium as solo founder is "hard mode." Need 325K free users at 2% conversion for $80K ARR. Staffing Studio needs 8-27 schools. "You don't need a new product. You need 10 schools."

Stair-step framework: Founder built a Step 2/3 product without completing Step 1 (learning to sell). The fix is sales, not a new product.

Price anchor: $8K/year minimum. "If you're solving a real staffing problem for a school managing a $2-5M personnel budget, $8K/year is a rounding error."

Sal Khan — Ed-Tech Visionary (Changed Vote)

Round 1: Advocated shifting to CVP. "Go where the families are. Build for families, institutions will follow." Projected $50-200K Year 1 revenue.

Round 2: Moved to CONDITIONAL (a). "I underweighted consumer distribution difficulty." Rob Walling's B2C math was persuasive.

Lingering challenge: "Seven of you voted to continue a product with 113K LOC and zero users. The absence of any pull after 11 milestones is itself a signal."
